01
CONSERVATION & COMPUTATION

Every cell must keep its accounts

Imagine drawing a fixed box in the flow. Fluid crosses its faces. Mass inside changes only when inflow and outflow differ.

PREDICT FIRSTWater enters a rigid, fully filled junction at 2 L/s. One branch takes 0.7 L/s. What must the other take?
The reasoning

1.3 L/s, assuming constant density and no other openings. This is a balance over all faces, not a rule that every face has the same velocity.

More in than out

Mass accumulates. In a fixed-volume gas, density can rise.

Equal in and out

The mass inside stays constant. Flow can still pass through rapidly.

Constant-density liquid

A rigid, fully filled box cannot accumulate mass. The total inflow must equal the total outflow.

ENGINEER’S HABIT

Check flow balance before interpreting colorful contours. A plausible picture does not establish conservation.

The equation, in plain language Optional depth
\[ \htmlClass{tm-time}{\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t}} + \htmlClass{tm-conv}{\nabla\!\cdot\!(\rho\,\mathbf{u})} = 0 \]
\(\partial\rho/\partial t\)
rate of change of density in the fixed box
\(\nabla\!\cdot\!(\rho\mathbf{u})\)
net mass flow out of the box per unit volume. "\(\nabla\cdot\)" (divergence, "div") means "out minus in, per unit volume"
\(\mathbf{u}\)
velocity vector, with parts \(u, v, w\) in \(x, y, z\)

Read it aloudThe rate at which mass builds up in the box, plus the net rate at which mass flows out of it, is zero.

Versteeg & Malalasekera (1995) Eq. 2.4, p. 12; Pope (2000) Eq. 2.17

\[ \htmlClass{tm-conv}{\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf{u}} = \htmlClass{tm-conv}{\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial w}{\partial z}} = 0 \]
\(\mathbf{u}\)
velocity vector (m/s)
\(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}\)
net volume outflow per unit volume (1/s)

Read it aloudIn a liquid, the volume that flows out of any box equals the volume that flows in. Speed up in one direction and you must slow down in another.

Versteeg & Malalasekera (1995) Eqs. 2.5–2.6, p. 13

Take a small box of fluid that does not move. The flow carries mass in through some faces and out through others. The mass in the box goes up by the difference. That is all of mass conservation.

02
CONSERVATION & COMPUTATION

Change can come from moving

A temperature probe can warm because warm fluid arrives. That does not mean each arriving fluid particle is warming.

PREDICT FIRSTA warm patch moves past a probe without diffusion. The probe warms and cools. Does the patch lose its temperature?
The reasoning

No. Each particle keeps its temperature in this ideal transport-only model. The fixed probe samples different particles.

Local change

What a fixed probe records over time.

Convective change

What a particle encounters because it moves through a spatial gradient.

Material change

The combination: what that moving particle actually experiences.

ENGINEER’S HABIT

Steady flow can accelerate fluid. Unsteady measurements can come from transport, even when each particle keeps its property.

The equation, in plain language Optional depth
\[ \frac{D\phi}{Dt} = \htmlClass{tm-time}{\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial t}} + \htmlClass{tm-conv}{\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\phi} \]
\(D\phi/Dt\)
rate of change of any quantity \(\phi\) seen by a moving particle (material derivative)
\(\partial\phi/\partial t\)
rate of change at a fixed point
\(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\phi\)
the change met by moving: speed times how fast \(\phi\) changes along the path

Read it aloudWhat the particle feels equals what a fixed probe sees, plus what the particle meets because it travels.

Versteeg & Malalasekera (1995) Eq. 2.7, p. 13; Pope (2000) Eq. 2.12, p. 13

Technical notes & course context Go further

Newton's law and the energy law are about a particle: a piece of fluid that moves. CFD needs them for a fixed box (A1, section 02). The link is the material derivative: the rate of change seen by a particle is the rate of change at a fixed point plus the change the particle meets because it moves.

Versteeg and Malalasekera then multiply by density and use mass conservation to write the same thing as "rate of increase in the box + net flow out of the box" (their Eq. 2.10, p. 14). That second form is the conservative form. CFD uses it, because it is a balance for a fixed box.

03
CONSERVATION & COMPUTATION

Navier–Stokes is a force balance

The momentum equation is Newton’s second law applied to fluid. Forces change its speed or direction.

PREDICT FIRSTIn a long, straight, fully developed channel, why can pressure keep dropping while speed no longer rises downstream?
The reasoning

The net pressure push balances wall friction. There is no streamwise acceleration in this ideal fully developed flow. Steady does not mean zero force; it means a balance.

Pressure

A pressure gradient produces a net force. Uniform pressure does not accelerate the fluid.

Viscous stress

Momentum transfer between neighboring regions. At walls, this produces friction.

Body forces

Forces acting throughout the fluid volume, such as gravity. These enter as sources or an equivalent pressure treatment.

ENGINEER’S HABIT

Ask which terms balance in your geometry before choosing schemes or trusting a solver result.

The equation, in plain language Optional depth
\[ \begin{aligned} &\htmlClass{tm-time}{\frac{\partial(\rho u)}{\partial t}} + \htmlClass{tm-conv}{\nabla\!\cdot\!(\rho u\,\mathbf{u})} \\ &\quad = \htmlClass{tm-press}{-\frac{\partial p}{\partial x}} + \htmlClass{tm-diff}{\nabla\!\cdot\!(\mu\,\nabla u)} + \htmlClass{tm-src}{S_{Mx}} \end{aligned} \]
\(\rho u\)
\(x\)-momentum per unit volume
\(\nabla\!\cdot\!(\rho u\mathbf{u})\)
\(x\)-momentum carried out of the box by the flow
\(-\partial p/\partial x\)
net pressure push in \(x\): from high pressure to low
\(\nabla\!\cdot\!(\mu\nabla u)\)
viscous friction: momentum spread by viscosity. "\(\nabla\)" (gradient, "grad") gives how fast \(u\) changes in each direction
\(S_{Mx}\)
momentum source: body forces, plus remaining viscous contributions in the general form

Read it aloudThe x-momentum in the box goes up, plus what the flow carries out, equals the pressure push plus the viscous friction plus any body force. The \(y\) and \(z\) equations have the same shape.

Versteeg & Malalasekera (1995) Eq. 2.37a, p. 24; the source \(S_M\) is defined in Eq. 2.33, p. 23

\[ \htmlClass{tm-time}{\frac{\partial \mathbf{u}}{\partial t}} + \htmlClass{tm-conv}{(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\,\mathbf{u}} = \htmlClass{tm-press}{-\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla p} + \htmlClass{tm-diff}{\nu\,\nabla^2\mathbf{u}} \]
\(\nabla^2\)
Laplacian: positive where a value is below the mean of its neighbours, so diffusion fills dips and flattens peaks. With a constant diffusion coefficient, diffusion appears as a Laplacian
\(p\)
here a modified pressure that also contains gravity's weight, so gravity has no separate term

Read it aloudAcceleration of the fluid equals the pressure push per unit mass plus viscous spreading of velocity. Together with \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u} = 0\), these are the Navier–Stokes equations for your water.

Pope (2000) Eqs. 2.34–2.35, p. 17. Gravity drops out into the pressure only when density is constant (Pope §2.5, p. 18).

Newton's second law for a particle: mass × acceleration = sum of forces. Versteeg and Malalasekera split the forces into two groups (1995, p. 15):

  • Surface forces act on the faces of the box: pressure and viscous stress.
  • Body forces act on all of the fluid inside: gravity, centrifugal and Coriolis forces, electromagnetic forces. CFD puts these in the source.

04
CONSERVATION & COMPUTATION

Carry heat. Spread heat. Add heat.

For the simple constant-property model here, temperature changes through convection, conduction, and any volumetric heating.

PREDICT FIRSTA rod heats the water only through its surface. Must the water also have a volumetric heat source?
The reasoning

No. The heated wall supplies a boundary heat flux or temperature condition. Adding the same power again as a fluid source would double-count it.

Convection

The moving fluid carries temperature patterns downstream.

Diffusion

Conduction spreads heat across temperature gradients.

Source or boundary

A heater inside the fluid is a source. A heated wall adds energy through the boundary.

ENGINEER’S HABIT

Always locate the energy input: inside the volume or across a boundary.

The equation, in plain language Optional depth
\[ \htmlClass{tm-time}{\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}} + \htmlClass{tm-conv}{\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla T} = \htmlClass{tm-diff}{\alpha\,\nabla^2 T} + \htmlClass{tm-src}{\frac{S}{\rho c}} \]
\(T\)
temperature (K)
\(\alpha\)
thermal diffusivity, \(k/(\rho c)\) (A1, section 05)
\(S\)
heat made inside the fluid per unit volume (W/m³); zero in your thesis water, which is heated only at the rod walls

Read it aloudTemperature at a point changes because the flow carries warm or cold fluid past it, because heat conducts from hot to cold, and because of any heat made inside.

Versteeg & Malalasekera (1995) Eq. 2.25, p. 20, with constant \(k\) and the viscous terms removed; Pope (2000) Eq. 2.54, p. 21 (the same equation as a passive scalar)

Technical notes & course context Go further

The energy law says: the energy of a particle goes up by the heat conducted in plus the work done on it. For a liquid with constant properties, Versteeg and Malalasekera reduce it to an equation for temperature (their Eq. 2.25). That equation also has a viscous heating term. 05
CONSERVATION & COMPUTATION

From one box to a whole mesh

A finite-volume solver repeats the same balance over every cell. Internal faces connect the neighboring accounts.

PREDICT FIRSTIf a face sends mass from cell A into cell B, should the two cells calculate unrelated face fluxes?
The reasoning

No. A conservative method uses a consistent shared flux: what leaves A enters B. Internal contributions then cancel in the domain-wide balance.

Cell total

What is stored inside this volume?

Face flux

How much crosses each face per unit time? A shared face leaves one cell and enters its neighbor.

Source

What is added or removed within this volume?

ENGINEER’S HABIT

Conservation gives a powerful check. It does not by itself prove mesh accuracy or validate the physical model.

The equation, in plain language Optional depth
\[ \begin{aligned} &\htmlClass{tm-time}{\frac{\partial(\rho\phi)}{\partial t}} + \htmlClass{tm-conv}{\nabla\!\cdot\!(\rho\phi\,\mathbf{u})} \\ &\quad = \htmlClass{tm-diff}{\nabla\!\cdot\!(\Gamma\,\nabla\phi)} + \htmlClass{tm-src}{S_\phi} \end{aligned} \]
\(\phi\)
transported quantity per unit mass
\(\Gamma\)
diffusion coefficient for that quantity
\(S_\phi\)
source per unit volume
\(\rho,\mathbf{u},t\)
density, velocity vector, time

Read it aloudThe amount of \(\phi\) in the box goes up, plus what the flow carries out, equals what diffusion brings in plus what is made inside.

Versteeg & Malalasekera (1995) Eq. 2.39, p. 25

\[ \begin{aligned} &\htmlClass{tm-time}{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{V}\rho\phi\,dV} + \htmlClass{tm-conv}{\oint_{A}\mathbf{n}\cdot(\rho\phi\,\mathbf{u})\,dA} \\ &\quad = \htmlClass{tm-diff}{\oint_{A}\mathbf{n}\cdot(\Gamma\nabla\phi)\,dA} + \htmlClass{tm-src}{\int_{V} S_\phi\,dV} \end{aligned} \]
\(\int_V\)
sum over the volume of the box
\(\oint_A\)
sum over all faces of the box
\(\mathbf{n}\)
unit vector normal to a face, pointing out

Read it aloudChange of the total in the box, plus the convective flux out through all faces, equals the diffusive flux in through all faces plus what is made inside. Only face fluxes and box totals remain.

Versteeg & Malalasekera (1995) Eqs. 2.40–2.42, pp. 25–26

06
CONSERVATION & COMPUTATION

Pressure makes the flow fit together

For constant-density flow, the unknowns are three velocity components and pressure. Continuity constrains how the velocities can fit together.

PREDICT FIRSTCan you solve temperature after the flow and still have a physically consistent model?
The reasoning

Yes, if temperature does not feed back through density, viscosity, buoyancy, or other coupled physics. That is the passive-temperature assumption, not a general property of heat transfer.

Momentum

Relates velocity to pressure, stresses, and forces.

Continuity

Requires the velocity field to conserve mass.

Pressure coupling

A pressure equation is obtained by combining these requirements. Pressure and velocity must be solved consistently.

ENGINEER’S HABIT

Count unknowns and equations, then check coupling and boundary conditions. The count alone does not guarantee a well-posed problem.

Technical notes & course context Go further

Counting equations and unknowns is a useful first check. Appropriate initial and boundary conditions and compatible equations are also necessary.

Pope (2000, §2.5) proves the pressure part. Take the divergence of the momentum equation and require \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u} = 0\) to stay true. This gives a Poisson equation for pressure, driven by the velocity field. So pressure "feels" the whole flow at once. Module D3 shows how OpenFOAM's SIMPLE and PIMPLE loops use this.

Versteeg and Malalasekera make the heat part: "Without density variations there is no linkage between the energy equation and the mass conservation and momentum equations." The energy equation is needed only "if the problem involves heat transfer" (1995, p. 21).

Why this matters for your thesis

This is why your proposal can solve the flow first with incompressibleFluid, then solve temperature on the converged, frozen flow with scalarTransport (§7.1). With constant properties and no buoyancy, temperature cannot change the flow. That is a modelling assumption, not a fact about water: real viscosity does change with temperature (A1, section 06). Your proposal (§7.1) estimates the error it causes in Nu.

CONSERVATION & COMPUTATION

Read fvSchemes as a physics map

The dictionary names are easier to remember once you can recognize the operation in the equation.

PREDICT FIRSTIs OpenFOAM’s phi always the generic transported quantity φ used in a textbook?
The reasoning

No. In this incompressible example, phi is volumetric face flux. The textbook φ is a placeholder for a transported quantity. The same name can mean different things.

ddt

Time change: how storage changes.

div

Divergence: net flux out. This is where convective terms commonly appear.

grad / laplacian

Gradients detect spatial differences. A Laplacian commonly represents constant-coefficient diffusion.

Choose a numerical scheme for an identified term. Avoid treating dictionaries as magic settings.

DM-08

Check yourself

A converged steady result has 1.0 kg/s entering and 0.9 kg/s leaving. Can steady storage explain the difference?

No. Steady storage is zero. Account for every opening and any allowed mass source; otherwise this is a mass-balance error.

A fluid particle warms as it moves through a steady temperature field. Does steady mean its material derivative is zero?

No. Local time change is zero, but convective change can be nonzero. The particle moves into a different temperature region.

A channel has a pressure gradient and constant downstream velocity profile. Must the pressure term be zero?

No. In fully developed straight-channel flow, the pressure term balances viscous effects. The acceleration vanishes because the forces balance.

For two neighboring cells, a shared-face flux contributes +3 to outward flow from one cell. What is its outward contribution to the other?

−3, with a consistent face orientation. The shared internal contribution cancels when the two balances are added.

Heating changes the water viscosity strongly and creates buoyancy. Can temperature still be treated as a passive field without checking the error?

No. These mechanisms feed temperature back into momentum. The one-way flow-then-temperature assumption needs reassessment; a coupled model may be required.

Words to keep

Open the chapter glossary
conservation law
A rule that a quantity (mass, momentum, energy) changes only by defined causes.
control volume, box
A fixed region of space that fluid flows through. A CFD cell is one.
rate of change term
How fast the amount in a fixed box changes. Zero in a steady flow.
convection
Transport of a quantity by the flow itself. (Some books say advection.)
diffusion
Spreading by molecular mixing, from high to low values. Viscosity for momentum, conduction for heat.
source
A quantity made or removed inside the box, for example a body force or a heater.
divergence (∇·, div)
Net flow out of a point, per unit volume.
gradient (∇, grad)
How fast a value changes in each direction.
Laplacian (∇²)
Positive where a value is below the mean of its neighbours. The form diffusion takes when its coefficient is constant.
material derivative (D/Dt)
Rate of change seen by a moving particle.
conservative form
An equation written as a balance for a fixed box: rate of change + net flux out = sources.
Navier–Stokes equations
The momentum equations for a fluid whose viscous stress follows Newton's law of viscosity.
surface force, body force
Forces on the faces of the box (pressure, viscous) and on the fluid inside it (gravity).
general transport equation
One equation shape that holds mass, momentum and temperature with different φ, Γ and S.
Gauss divergence theorem
A divergence summed over a volume equals the flux summed over its faces.
flux
The amount of a quantity that crosses a face per unit time.
Poisson equation for pressure
The equation that fixes pressure from the velocity field, so that mass stays conserved.
phi
OpenFOAM's name for the flux through each cell face.
